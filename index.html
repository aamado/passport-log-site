Passport Log

Counts your days in every country, automatically.

Your iPhone already knows when you cross a border. Passport Log turns that into a day-by-day record of the countries you have been in — for watching tax residency thresholds, building a residency or citizenship history, or simply keeping track of where you have been.

Download on the App Store

Free. Requires iPhone with iOS 18 or later.

How it works

  • Records your country in the background, using a low-power service that wakes the app only when you move a significant distance.
  • Works out the country on the phone itself, from a map of 249 countries and territories built into the app, so it works with no signal and no roaming data.
  • Counts any part of a day, in the local time zone of wherever you were. Cross a border and the day counts for both countries.
  • Fills in quiet days from the days around them, and asks you about days where the country changed while it wasn't looking.
  • Knows the difference between visiting a country and flying over it: only the country you took off from and the one you landed in count, while a stop between two flights still counts for where you stopped.
  • Lets you add past trips and correct any day by hand.

Day limits

Set a limit for a country — 183 days, say — counted over a calendar year or the last twelve months. Passport Log shows how many days are left and the date you would reach the limit if you stayed put, and warns you thirty and ten days before, and on the day itself.

Export

Export your history as a CSV file, one row per day and country, marked according to whether the day was recorded, filled in or added by hand. Useful for an accountant, or for your own records.

Your data stays yours

There is no server and no account. Everything stays on your iPhone and, if you want, in your own private iCloud, which the developer cannot read. Nothing is sent to the developer, there is no advertising or tracking of any kind, and you can delete everything in one go.

Why iCloud?

Because a record built up over years is not something you can reconstruct from memory. Syncing keeps your days on this iPhone and in your own private iCloud, so they survive a lost, broken or replaced phone and come back when you restore it. Without it, your history lives on one device and goes with it.

It is Apple's iCloud under your own account — not a service the developer runs, and not somewhere anyone else can look. It is a switch in Settings, so you can turn it off and keep everything on the one device instead.

Free

Passport Log is free, with no ads and nothing to buy.

What's next

Ideas for future versions, in no particular order and with no dates attached. If one of these matters to you, say so — that is the best way to move it up the list.

  • A choice of counting rules. Today every country counts any part of a day. Some count only days you were present at midnight, others only days with an overnight stay. Picking the rule that matches your situation is the most requested change.
  • Schengen 90/180. The Schengen area counted as one zone over a rolling 180 days.
  • States and provinces. US states, Canadian provinces and the like, for state-level tax rules.
  • Special territories. Places with their own tax rules inside a country, such as the Canary Islands or Madeira.
  • A PDF report. A formatted day count for an accountant or an authority.
  • Your travel, in numbers. What the record adds up to: days per country each year, your longest unbroken stay, this year against last. Worked out on your phone from what is already there — these are insights for you, not information about you going anywhere.
  • A passport of stamps. A page you can leaf through with a stamp for every country you have been in, its first and last visit and the days you spent there — and a spread you can save or share. The screen the app's name has been promising.
  • Importing flight history, so past trips do not have to be typed in by hand.
  • More notifications. Today the app only speaks up about a day limit. It could also tell you when you enter a new country, when a day needs reviewing, or how your year is going. Scheduled on the phone like the warnings already are, so nothing is sent from a server.
  • More languages. Portuguese first.
  • An Apple Watch app, for the current country and limit status at a glance.
  • An Android version. Under consideration, not started.

Because Passport Log keeps every raw location reading rather than only the daily totals, most of these can be applied to history you have already recorded, rather than only to days from then on.
